## The defect this round started from

Three debts, and every one of them was a **claim that nothing checked**:

- The merge audit existed and the gate ran its TEST — the fixtures. The
  check's guarantee ("every merged PR has a `pr_merged` event") was never
  exercised against what it was written to catch, and the audit had been
  failing on live data since the day it landed.
- The round cards said how many commits their rounds had. Both numbers were
  short, because the count was written when the slice was cut and the later
  fix commits were never counted.
- The Russian mirror was behind the original, and nothing compared the two
  files, so the drift was invisible.

A check whose subject is a claim must run against the claim. All three
debts were the same defect wearing different clothes: **a number nobody
re-measured**.

## The three slices

### A — the merge trail is audited on the real remote (`b231abd`)

`scripts/merge-audit-baseline.tsv` gains the third stratum: PRs 61-71, each
with the reason it has no event. The audit SCRIPT now runs in the gate as
its own layer (`merge-audit`), not only its test.

The layer's honest edge is `--skip-if-unavailable`. A CI runner has neither
gh's credentials nor the pair's state directory — the journal is gitignored,
so there is nothing to audit there — and a permanent red would teach the
reader to ignore the layer. So the skip is loud ("SKIPPED: gh is not
installed: the merged PRs cannot be read") and covers only what could not be
READ. A merge that WAS read and has no event still fails, and that is
tested: the flag changes the exit code of a blind run, never of a seeing one.

Live result on the merged tree: 96 merged PRs, 26 with a merge record, 70
without, all 70 named. PASS.

### B — the cards say what they mean (`facf528`)

`docs/rounds/146.md` and `docs/rounds/147.md` carry their round's commits in
the order the PR held them, each with the section it landed in, taken from
the PR itself (`gh api repos/AVP-Dev/pair-mailbox/pulls/{95,96}/commits`)
rather than from the card's own memory.

Both cards now say the merge was a squash and that the rollback is "revert
THE MERGE" — which is what a reader can actually do. Round 147's plan declared
five slices and the card described four: slice E is the card itself
(`9b9b95d`), and it is named as such.

Round 147's card also reported the mirror's gap as "§14-16 and §17". The
card now names the three gaps that were real then and are real now, and says
where §14-16 stand.

### C — the mirror has the original's structure (`8933338`)

The three missing blocks are translated in the mirror's own voice and
formatting: `## 9b` (a note's kind is its author's namespace, and writing
into the other side's kind file is how a forgery stops being detectable),
`## 17` (the four `pairup`/`pairctl worktree` commands), and the §6 plan-gate
subsection (the machine gate that guards the forward move — half of §6).

The mirror has 24 sections and 36 subsections, the same as the original, in
the same order.

`scripts/protocol_parity_check.py` compares the two SKELETONS: every numbered
section and every subsection, by number, in order. The prose is a translation
and cannot be compared mechanically; the skeleton can, and the skeleton is
what a reader navigates by. The section NUMBERS are not renumbered when one
is missing in the original — the mirror mirrors the original, so a gap in the
original is a gap in the mirror too. That is a property, not a convenience:
the check would otherwise push for a section the English original does not
have.

## The honest limits

- **The audit still depends on `gh` and on this project's journal.** The
  layer is loud when it cannot look, but it cannot prove anything on a
  machine that has neither. A journal from another branch's pair would be a
  DIFFERENT trail, and the check would read the eleven as escapes again —
  which is the correct answer for that journal, and the reason the baseline
  names them rather than pretending they never happened.
- **The parity check compares structure, not prose.** A translated section
  whose sentences drifted from the original still passes. Comparing prose
  across languages mechanically would need an embedding, and a check that
  guesses would be worse than one that does not.
- **§14-16 are still missing from BOTH files.** Renumbering the protocol is a
  change to the original's structure, not a translation task, and it moves
  every cross-reference in both files. It needs its own round; this one
  leaves it open and says so.
- **The cards' numbers are right as of this round.** The count is a fact
  about a round that has happened, and it cannot rot — but a card written
  before its round's last fix commit WILL be short, which is exactly the
  defect this round fixes. The rule for the next card: take the count from
  the PR, not from memory.

## Rollback

Reverting THE MERGE removes the three debts' fixes: the eleven rows from the
baseline, the `merge-audit` layer and its `--skip-if-unavailable` flag, the

the baseline and the cards are data the audit reads, and the parity check has
one caller — the gate.
